Overview

Sand Blocks is a portrait drag puzzle with 500 levels. Glass tanks at the top of the board hold coloured sand in layers; every tank column pours its bottom layer straight down into the box that rests under it. Drag the open boxes on the board (they slide through free cells only) under a column whose bottom sand matches their colour: the sand pours in, the full box pops and frees its cells. Fill every box before the countdown reaches 00:00 to win; at 00:00 the level is lost. Level 1 is a guided tutorial with the shared tutorial hand.

Mechanics introduced over the campaign: layered tank columns (level 5), numbered covers that open after that many boxes have been filled (level 10), boxes hidden under closed covers (level 15), key boxes that open the padlocked box (level 40) and pipe-fed tanks (level 60), on nine board families (twin tanks, corridors, steps, pillars, channels, covered boards, towers, pockets and vaults). Every 50th level is a SUPER HARD level. Levels 1 and 200 are the two recorded boards of the original game.

The HyperCasualLiveOpsKit (separate root Assets/HyperCasualLiveOpsKit, with its own guide) supplies the menu, level selection, capsule HUD, results, wallet, quests, pass, minigames, shop (LocalQA dummy store), ads (LocalQA mock) and the booster bar with refills.

Getting Started

  1. Create a Unity 6000.0.62f1 project with the Universal Render Pipeline, the Input System (set Active Input Handling to Both or Input System) and uGUI; import TextMesh Pro Essential Resources when Unity asks.
  2. Import the package. Assign Assets/Game/ProjectRenderSettings/PC_RPAsset.asset (or Mobile_RPAsset.asset) in Graphics and Quality settings and use the Linear colour space.
  3. Add Assets/HyperCasualLiveOpsKit/StarterMenu/Scenes/HLK_MainMenu.unity (first) and Assets/Game/Scenes/Game.unity to the build settings and press Play in the menu scene. Choose a level or press PLAY NEXT LEVEL.
  4. Open Tools → Sand Blocks → Level Editor or Auto Level Generator to inspect and change the campaign, and Tools → Sand Blocks → Documentation for this guide.

Gameplay

Moving. Press a visible box and drag: it follows the finger through free cells (it collides with other boxes, walls, closed covers and the board edge) and snaps to the nearest cell on release. One box is held at a time. A box under a closed cover cannot be picked up; a press on it counts as a blocked attempt.

Pouring. Each tank column pours its bottom sand layer into the box on its dock cell (the cell below the column; the outermost columns also feed the cell beside the tank's bottom row). The colour must match. A box fills at the level's fillPctPerSec, split over the columns feeding it, and the sand leaves those columns' queues; when a layer runs out the next colour above it reaches the bottom.

Covers, keys, padlock. A cover shows a number: every filled box lowers it by one; at zero the cover rolls away and the boxes under it can be moved. Filling a key box sends its key to the padlocked box; when all keys have arrived, the padlock opens and that box can receive sand.

Outcome. Filling every box wins (the kit results credit the win reward once, default 50 coins). The countdown (90–240 s per level) reaching 00:00 loses; the lose card offers TRY AGAIN. Every campaign level has a stored solution (witness) that wins in the simulation with at least 61 s to spare.

Level Editor

Tools → Sand Blocks → Level Editor lists the campaign on the left (duplicate, delete, move up/down, Undo). The canvas draws the board as the game lays it out: tanks with their sand layers (the bottom layer pours first), dock marks under each tank column, board cells, walls, numbered covers and every box in its colour and footprint with key/padlock marks.

  • Box: pick a footprint (1x1, 1x2, 1x3, 1x4, 2x2, L3, L4), a quarter turn, mirror and colour, then click a free cell; click a box to remove it.
  • Cell adds/removes board cells (notches, bays), Wall toggles a wall on an empty board cell, Cover adds/removes a cell of the selected cover and sets its count, Key/Lock cycles a box through key → padlocked → plain.
  • Rebuild sand + witness re-derives the tank layers and a witness for the edited board with the generator's construction (proven on the Sim; try another seed when it fails); Solve searches a witness for the current data with the policy solver; Check validates and replays it.

Every edit clears the level's stored witness. Saving refuses levels without a proven witness, invalid layouts and duplicate puzzles, and leaves the published JSON unchanged on failure. Play saved level opens it in the game scene.

Automatic Level Generator

Tools → Sand Blocks → Auto Level Generator (same window) saves the generation request (final count, seed, attempts, minimum formations, repeat streak, append) and generates into the editor for review before saving. Generation is deterministic per seed and slot: each board is built forward from its solution — the generator lays out the family's tanks and board, places the boxes, then plays the planned delivery order to derive which sand each column holds, so every board is solvable by construction and its witness is replayed on the Sim. Slots 1 and 200 keep the recorded boards. Append mode keeps the existing levels and adds new ones after them; cancelling leaves the campaign unchanged. A 500-level run takes about three minutes in the editor.

Boosters

BoosterUnlockEffect
FreezeLevel 2Stops the countdown for 10 s.
HammerLevel 7Tap a visible box: it fills at once; its sand is taken from the tanks.
UFOLevel 12Tap a visible box: every box of that colour fills and that colour's sand leaves the tanks.

Each unlock grants two free charges once. Refills cost 100 + 25 × floor((highest level − 1) / 10) coins or a rewarded ad (LocalQA mock). The policy is Game/Resources/HLK/BoosterProgression.json. The first use shows the booster explanation; a targeted booster then guides its first tap with the shared tutorial hand on a valid box. Taps on covered boxes are not targets and cost nothing.

Level Variation

Families: twin tanks (from level 2), corridor (3), step (5), pillar (7), channels (10), covered boards (15), towers (20), pocket (25) and vault (40). The opening 2–40 introduces one idea at a time; afterwards a seeded schedule never shows a family three times running. Boxes grow from 2–6 per board in levels 1–10 to about 12 on average late in the campaign (up to 28), colours from 2 to 9. Levels ending in 8 (from 28) are lighter recovery boards; every 50th level is SUPER HARD. A rectangle-silhouette rule shapes every third board (narrow tanks, notched corners, comb walls). The current campaign has 320 distinct geometries; the historical variety audit (path silhouettes 124, combined structures 325, no similar board or path run longer than two) passed.

Customization Guide

  • Timing and rewards: the countdown per level is each level's timerSeconds; the kit's win reward is StarterMenuConfig.WinCoinReward (the level's own reward is used only by the game's standalone result screen).
  • Pour speed: each level's fillPctPerSec (percent of a box per second).
  • Board framing: Viewport.MaxCellFraction and Viewport.SolveHost; the area is SandBlocksHlkBridge.PlayArea().
  • Colours: the palette is in LevelBuilder.PaletteHex and the per-level palette table; box and sand art comes from the existing models and materials.
  • Progression: edit CampaignPlan.Introductions, the opening table or Plan, then regenerate with the tools (the recorded slots are preserved).
  • Boosters: unlock levels, grants and prices in BoosterProgression.json.

Level Format

Each Level_N.json holds the grid (cols, rows), the playable board cells and walls, the boxes (pieces: id, colour, absolute cells, footprint shape, rot, mirror, optional key / locks), the tanks (reservoirs: cells, pipe, and per column the sand queue from the bottom layer up as [colour, box cells]), the covers (cells and count), the countdown, pour speed, palette, the board family, the slot index and the proven witness. Safe worked editing procedure

  1. Open Level 4 in the Level Editor and duplicate it into an unused slot.
  2. Use the editor’s supported controls for a small placement change; keep legal occupancy and required targets. Do not invent JSON fields.
  3. Run the shipped validator, correct any rule errors, save, then open the duplicate in gameplay and complete it.
