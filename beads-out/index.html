Overview

Beads Out is a portrait bead-sorting puzzle with 500 levels. A conveyor belt of beads (80 rows of six) turns around a loop past a spout. Tap a box in the tray: it flies to the lowest free shelf slot, and every belt row of its colour that passes the spout pours into it. A full box flies away and frees its slot. Side feeder arms drop their queued rows into empty belt rows. Fill every box to win. If the shelf is full and nothing pours or enters the belt for a whole lap, the level offers one recovery (the newest box returns to the tray); the next stall loses. Level 1 (the tutorial) and level 200 are the two recorded boards of the original game; the other 498 are generated from a saved seed.

Getting Started

  1. Create or open a Unity 6000.0.62f1 project that uses the Universal Render Pipeline, the Input System package (activeInputHandler = Input System or Both), Unity UI and the Test Framework. Import TextMesh Pro Essential Resources when prompted.
  2. Import this package. Two roots appear: Assets/Game (game, scene, levels, tools) and Assets/HyperCasualLiveOpsKit (menu, economy, LiveOps, boosters).
  3. Assign a URP pipeline asset in Graphics/Quality settings (the tested PC/Mobile assets are in Assets/Game/ProjectRenderSettings) and set Player → Color Space to Linear.
  4. Add Assets/HyperCasualLiveOpsKit/StarterMenu/Scenes/HLK_MainMenu.unity first and Assets/Game/Scenes/Game.unity second to the build list, open the menu scene and press Play. Choose a level or press Play Next Level.
  5. Open Tools → Beads Out → Documentation for this guide, Level Editor / Auto Level Generator for the tools.

Gameplay

Two tracks come from the recordings: the tall Level 1 track (no feeders) and the wide Level 200 track with two feeder arms. The loop itself is level data: a level either keeps the recorded ring or stores its own closed path, one of 13 silhouettes (rounded square, round, broad low loop, hourglass, tilted pear, rounded triangle, domed/flat, kidney, leaning, rounded diamond, D, heart, S-bend). The same belt rules apply on every loop - 80 rows of six beads, one spout under the bottom, feeder arms joining on their own side - and the loop is drawn with the same procedural channel, rims and chevrons. Trays are stepped stacks of one to five box rows on one shared column grid: stacks that narrow downwards are centred (the two recorded trays), other row profiles are left-aligned. A chain (padlock) covers the bottom row of a tray, or its bottom two rows from four-row trays, and opens 1.25 s after its key box is sent. Losing opportunities come from feeder-only colours: a colour that starts only in a feeder arrives after other colours empty belt rows, so parking it early fills the shelf. Tutorials point the kit's shared tutorial hand at the guided box, with a one-line instruction in a capsule above the board (level 1 boxes, level 8 side belts, level 46 key). Pause, settings, results, rewards and the booster shelf are the kit's; win results appear after the game's own completion celebration.

Level Editor

Tools → Beads Out → Level Editor lists the campaign with Undo, duplicate, delete and reorder, a spatial canvas and an exact JSON view. The canvas draws the loop on its real centreline with flow arrows, every starting belt row in its colour, the feeder queues and their junctions, the shelf slots and the tray boxes with the key icon and chain. Tools: paint a box, paint five belt rows, move a box into or out of the chained zone, choose the key box; pick the tray formation, the shelf size (3-5 slots), the box capacity (20 or 10 rows) and the loop (a catalog preset, copied into this level only). The Loop points tool drags the loop's control points, double-click on the loop inserts one, right-click removes one, shift-click moves that side's feeder junction; the arms re-attach after every edit and a warning names any loop rule that fails. Edits clear the witness; saving re-solves, validates bead conservation, overlaps and the campaign rules, and publishes through staging. Play opens the saved level through the normal menu flow.

Automatic Level Generator

Tools → Beads Out → Auto Level Generator uses the saved request (count, seed, attempts, minimum formations, streak limit, append/replace). Generation is deterministic: the same request yields the same campaign. Each candidate is built from its chapter blueprint, solved, replayed through the Sim and checked for duplicates, formation streaks and chapter rules; the loop silhouette follows its own seeded schedule (recorded ring at levels 1, 8 and 200, the reference shapes at levels 27, 42, 45, 48 and 78, otherwise no silhouette again within two levels); bounded retries never weaken constraints, and a failure leaves the published JSON unchanged. Append mode keeps every existing level and adds new ones to the target count.

Boosters

BoosterEffectUnlock / free charges
Extra SlotAdds one shelf slot for this level (up to six).Level 2 · 2 once

FillPulls matching beads from the belt and feeders into the first unfinished shelf box.Level 12 · 2 once

The effects are the V2 developer's; the kit supplies inventory, first-use tutorials, arming, audio and DEV unlock/refill/tutorial controls.

Progression and refills. A booster unlocks permanently when a real win makes its level reachable (completing level 1 unlocks Extra Slot for level 2) and stays usable on earlier replays; on that first unlock it grants two free charges, once per booster and never per level. Each successful effect costs one charge; a refused, cancelled or invalid use costs nothing. At zero the button shows a gray icon with a white 0 and opens the kit's GET ANOTHER BOOST popup: BUY +1 for 100 coins plus 25 per 10 levels of real progress (no replay discount), or WATCH AD +1 on the booster's rewarded placement (booster_refill_<id>). A refill adds one charge and closes the popup without firing the booster; skipped, failed or unavailable ads grant nothing, and every purchase is journaled so an interrupted transaction is settled exactly once on the next launch. DEV level jumps and DEV refills never grant charges or record progress. Inventory is saved per profile (HLK.beads-out.<profile>.Booster.*); older saved counts are migrated, not reset.

Every level ending in 5 from 25 is a lighter relief level. From level 21 the box and colour counts rise toward those seen in later levels of the original game, up to 20 boxes and 13 colours in the Master chapter, with belt colour runs of 5-10 rows. The shipped campaign has 499 distinct tray/track/chain/feeder/loop formations (378 tray row profiles) and never repeats a formation on consecutive levels (the generator allows at most two in a row). The loop varies separately: all 13 silhouettes appear in every chapter from level 21, a silhouette never returns within two levels, and every 20-level window shows at least 11 of them.

Customization Guide

  • Difficulty: edit BeadsProgression.Chapters (boxes, colours, feeder-only share, chain chance, shelf and capacity mixes, run lengths) and regenerate with a new seed, or hand-edit levels in the editor.
  • Colours: BeadsBoards.Colours / Hex list the thirteen recorded bead colours; look-alike families are kept apart early.
  • Tray shapes: a tray is a row profile (TrayShape, boxes per row, e.g. 5-5-4-2). BeadsBoards.Shapes enumerates the profiles each track allows from its measured grid (TallGrid: up to 2 rows / 8 boxes, WideGrid: up to 5 rows / 20 boxes); ChainRows sets the chained rows. New profiles raise formation variety automatically.
  • Loops: reshape one level with the Loop points tool, or add a silhouette to BeadsPathCatalog.Families (40 column u,v control points per track); BeadsPaths.Check accepts it when it bends no tighter than the channel, keeps 94-112% of the recorded loop length (same rows and bead size), stays inside the recorded track band and leaves room for both feeder arms. BeadsPaths.Schedule places the silhouettes in the campaign.
  • Boosters: unlock levels, the one-time grant, refill amount, prices and rewarded placements live in Game/Resources/HLK/BoosterProgression.json (the file the player loads; it sets each definition's unlock level at start-up and is validated against the power-up catalog). Names, icons and tutorials are in the definition assets (Game/Integration/PowerUps); effects in Sim.AddSlot, Sim.TapBox (Move any) and Sim.FillBlock. The kit's PowerUps/BOOSTER-PROGRESSION.md and BOOSTER-REFILL-UI.md describe the shared policy and popup.
  • Rewards, ads, LiveOps: see the HyperCasualLiveOpsKit guide.
  • DEV panel: the orange DEV button is a development tool; hide it for store builds with StarterGameplayDevPanel.IsAvailable = false.

Level Format

Each Level_N.json holds the track data (column size, ring rectangle, spout, feeder arm paths and junction rows), the optional loop ring.path (id = catalog silhouette, a trailing * marks a hand-edited copy; points = closed control points as column fractions; absent = the recorded ring), the palette, ring.rows0 (80 belt rows, clockwise from the top; null = empty), each feeder's rows0 queue (first row nearest the junction), box.capacityRows, the shelf slot rectangles, the tray (plate, cut-out with one rectangle per row block, boxes with grid cell, rectangle, colour, locked, key), the optional lock, the tutorial (guided box ids, caption, hint), HUD/result data, track (tall or wide), plus index and the witness (step:boxId taps on the 100 Hz clock). Safe worked editing procedure

  1. Open Level 4 in the Level Editor and duplicate it into an unused slot.
  2. Use the editor’s supported controls for a small placement change; keep legal occupancy and required targets. Do not invent JSON fields.
  3. Run the shipped validator, correct any rule errors, save, then open the duplicate in gameplay and complete it.
  4. For generation, record the current seed, request a small append batch using the exposed controls, validate its solutions and confirm the previous campaign prefix is unchanged. Cancelling must leave the campaign unchanged.
